# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

The imPress project is a shared space for people building and maintaining a
classroom response system. We want participation to be safe, respectful,
constructive, and accessible to people with different backgrounds, identities,
experience levels, abilities, and communication styles.

By participating in this repository, its issues, pull requests, discussions,
documentation, events, or other project spaces, you agree to act in ways that
support this commitment. This policy applies to maintainers, contributors,
reviewers, users, and visitors equally.

## Expected behavior

Project participants are expected to:

- Treat other people with respect, patience, and good faith.
- Discuss ideas and evidence rather than attacking or stereotyping people.
- Give actionable, technically specific feedback; distinguish a defect in
  code from the worth or ability of its author.
- Welcome questions and help newcomers find relevant documentation or
  context, without expecting everyone to know unwritten project history.
- Use clear, inclusive language. Ask respectfully when a name, pronoun,
  terminology, or accessibility preference is not known.
- Respect different cultural, educational, professional, and communication
  backgrounds. Disagreement is welcome; hostility and personal contempt are
  not.
- Keep discussions focused on the project and move sensitive or personal
  matters to an appropriate private channel.
- Credit others' ideas and work, respect licenses, and be honest about what
  was tested, reviewed, or verified.
- Protect the privacy and safety of students, teachers, operators, and
  contributors. Do not disclose personal data, credentials, access tokens,
  private keys, private messages, or identifiable classroom records.
- Follow the project's security reporting guidance: do not publish actionable
  exploit instructions or sensitive vulnerability details before a coordinated
  fix or disclosure decision.
- Accept maintainer decisions and enforcement actions respectfully. If you
  disagree, use the reporting or appeal process below instead of retaliating.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes, but is not limited to:

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking, threats, or deliberate disruption.
- Insults, slurs, demeaning stereotypes, or hostile conduct directed at a
  person's identity, background, disability, appearance, or experience.
- Sexualized language or attention, unwanted advances, or sexual imagery in
  project spaces.
- Repeated unwanted contact after someone asks for it to stop.
- Publishing or threatening to publish someone's private information
  (doxxing), credentials, private communications, or other identifying details.
- Retaliating against someone for raising a concern, participating in an
  investigation, or receiving an enforcement action.
- Knowingly misrepresenting test results, review status, security properties,
  or another contributor's work.
- Sabotaging branches, CI, releases, project systems, or another contributor's
  work; deliberately exposing users to unsafe behavior; or using project
  access to harm, intimidate, or exploit others.
- Persistently derailing technical discussions, flooding issues or review
  threads, or interfering with moderation after a clear request to stop.
- Encouraging, coordinating, or assisting any of the above.

Firm and respectful criticism of code, product decisions, security claims, or
project process is not harassment by itself. Raise it with evidence, keep it
relevant, and avoid personal attacks.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project-operated or project-related spaces,
including:

- GitHub repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and project
  boards;
- project chat, mailing lists, meetings, demonstrations, and events;
- public communication when a person is acting as a project representative or
  when their conduct materially affects the safety or participation of the
  project community.

Maintainers may clarify whether a situation falls within this scope. A personal
disagreement unrelated to the project should not be used as a pretext for
project sanctions.

## Reporting a concern

If you experience or observe unacceptable behavior:

1. If there is immediate danger, prioritize personal safety and contact the
   appropriate local emergency service. The project is not an emergency
   response organization.
2. For a project concern, contact a repository maintainer privately through
   GitHub. Do not open a public issue containing private information,
   credentials, or security-sensitive details.
3. Provide the relevant link, approximate time, people involved if known, what
   happened, and any context or evidence you are comfortable sharing. Share
   only information necessary to understand the concern.
4. State whether you want a response, a private conversation, or a particular
   safety measure. You may ask a trusted person to help you prepare or submit
   the report.

If the report concerns a maintainer, send it to another repository owner or
maintainer who is not involved. If there is no independent maintainer
available, use GitHub's reporting channels and avoid posting sensitive
information publicly.

Reporters are not expected to investigate, confront the person involved, or
collect evidence at personal risk. Do not access another person's account or
device, or retain personal data, to substantiate a report.

## How reports are handled

Maintainers will acknowledge reports as soon as reasonably practical, assess
immediate safety or privacy risks, and decide what additional information is
needed. The person handling a report should:

- treat the report seriously and fairly;
- limit access to report details to people who need them to respond;
- avoid conflicts of interest and hand the matter to an independent maintainer
  when necessary;
- give the person who is the subject of a report an appropriate opportunity to
  respond when doing so is safe and does not expose the reporter or compromise
  an investigation;
- protect reporters and witnesses from retaliation;
- share only the outcome and details necessary for safety, accountability, or
  project operations.

Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised when a report must be shared to
protect people, comply with law, or conduct a fair investigation. Maintainers
will make reasonable efforts to respect privacy and to explain any necessary
sharing.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take the action they judge proportionate to the behavior,
severity, pattern, impact, and safety risk. Possible actions include:

1. **Guidance:** a private or public request to stop a behavior, clarify the
   expectation, or repair a discussion.
2. **Warning:** a formal notice that specific conduct violated this policy and
   that repeated behavior may lead to restrictions.
3. **Content moderation:** editing or removing offending material, limiting
   replies, locking a thread, or redirecting a discussion.
4. **Temporary restriction:** a time-limited restriction on posting, reviewing,
   participating in events, or using project-managed resources.
5. **Removal:** removal from a project space, role, event, or repository
   access, temporarily or permanently.

In a serious or urgent situation, maintainers may take an immediate protective
action before completing a full investigation. The action should be reviewed
when practical. Enforcement is about safety and the health of the project; it
is not a public contest or an invitation to retaliate.

## Review and appeal

A person affected by an enforcement action may request a review by privately
contacting a maintainer who was not the sole decision-maker. The request should
explain what decision is being challenged and provide any relevant context.
Another maintainer will review the decision when one is reasonably available.
An appeal does not automatically suspend a safety restriction. The reviewer
may affirm, change, or reverse the action and will communicate the decision
privately when appropriate.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for setting a respectful example, explaining
project rules, moderating project spaces, responding to reports, and applying
this policy consistently. They should not use their role to target critics,
settle personal disputes, or demand private information unrelated to a
legitimate project or safety need.

## Questions and updates

For questions about how this policy applies, contact a repository maintainer
privately through GitHub. This policy may be updated as the project and its
community evolve; participants are expected to follow the current published
version.
