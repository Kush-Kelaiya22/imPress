# Code of Conduct

**Project:** imPress
**Applies to:** Project maintainers, contributors, reviewers, collaborators, users, and participants in project-related spaces
**Status:** Community policy

## 1. Purpose and commitment

imPress is a classroom response system developed and improved through collaborative software engineering. The project brings together people who design, implement, test, document, deploy, review, and use its software. Effective collaboration requires technical rigor, accountability, respectful communication, and care for the people affected by the system.

We are committed to maintaining an environment in which people can participate without harassment, intimidation, discrimination, or retaliation. We welcome contributions from people with different backgrounds, identities, abilities, levels of experience, and approaches to problem-solving. Participation should be determined by conduct and the quality of collaboration, not by seniority, familiarity with the maintainers, or personal relationships.

This Code of Conduct sets expectations for interaction, identifies conduct that is not acceptable, and explains how concerns will be reviewed. It applies equally to the people who originally built imPress and those who become involved later. Holding repository access, making a major contribution, or maintaining a fork does not grant special exemptions from these standards.

The goal is not to prevent criticism, disagreement, or difficult technical decisions. It is to ensure that such discussions remain constructive, evidence-based, proportionate, and safe for participants.

## 2. Guiding principles

Our community operates according to the following principles:

- **Respect:** Treat people with dignity, including when their ideas, code, or decisions are being challenged.
- **Technical integrity:** State facts accurately, document assumptions, and distinguish verified results from expectations or opinions.
- **Fair participation:** Make room for newcomers and less experienced contributors without lowering reasonable standards for security, correctness, or quality.
- **Accountability:** Own mistakes, respond to feedback, and correct problems without assigning personal blame unnecessarily.
- **Privacy and safety:** Protect individuals and institutions whose information may be handled by imPress or discussed in project spaces.
- **Transparency with discretion:** Document technical decisions openly when practical, while keeping private reports and sensitive information restricted.
- **Consistency:** Apply community standards based on behavior, context, impact, and evidence rather than popularity or project status.
- **Responsible stewardship:** Favor maintainable changes, reproducible results, and an understandable project history over shortcuts that shift risks onto others.

## 3. Who is covered

This policy governs the conduct of anyone participating in spaces operated, moderated, or officially represented by the imPress project. This includes maintainers, code owners, contributors, issue reporters, pull-request authors, reviewers, translators, documentation writers, testers, security researchers, deployers, and users.

Participants remain responsible for their actions when interacting on behalf of an employer, educational institution, student group, company, or other organization. An affiliation does not reduce individual responsibility or override this policy within project-controlled spaces.

People who have not made a code contribution are also participants when they engage through issues, discussions, documentation, support requests, demonstrations, or other recognized project channels.

## 4. Where the policy applies

This Code of Conduct applies to:

1. The official imPress repository and associated repositories, including issues, pull requests, code reviews, commit discussions, wikis, releases, and project boards.
2. Project-operated discussion forums, mailing lists, chats, communication channels, meetings, workshops, demonstrations, and events.
3. Official documentation, support channels, and other collaboration resources maintained by the project.
4. Public or private communications made in an official project capacity, including statements made while representing the project or exercising delegated project authority.
5. Conduct outside project-operated spaces when there is a clear, substantial connection to participation in imPress and the conduct creates a credible safety risk, targeted harassment, or serious interference with the project community.

Maintainers will not treat unrelated private disagreements as automatic grounds for action. When evaluating conduct outside official spaces, they should identify the connection to the project, consider available evidence, and avoid unnecessary intrusion into personal matters.

### 4.1 Clones, forks, modifications, and downstream deployments

imPress may be cloned, modified, redistributed, self-hosted, or used as part of another system, subject to the applicable software license and other legal obligations. We encourage everyone who uses or adapts the project to uphold the standards described here, especially when interacting with contributors or handling classroom data.

The following distinctions are important:

- **Contributions to the official project:** Anyone who submits changes, reports issues, seeks support, or interacts in official imPress spaces must comply with this policy, regardless of whether they work from a fork or a clone.
- **Officially affiliated forks and deployments:** Spaces and activities presented as part of the official imPress project, or operated under its authority, are covered to the extent that the project controls or moderates them.
- **Independent forks and deployments:** Independent maintainers and operators are strongly encouraged to adopt this Code of Conduct, or an equivalent policy, for their own communities. The imPress maintainers cannot automatically moderate independent spaces or impose community sanctions there merely because the software originated here.
- **Redistributed copies:** Including this policy in a redistributed copy communicates the project's standards; it does not, by itself, transfer authority over an independent project to the original imPress maintainers.
- **Software licensing:** Permission to copy, use, modify, or distribute the code is determined by the project's applicable license, not by this Code of Conduct. This policy does not add new conditions to that license or revoke permissions it grants.

If an independent fork or deployment engages with official project resources, its representatives are subject to this policy for those interactions. Project maintainers may restrict access to official collaboration channels and project-controlled resources when necessary, but they do not gain control over independently operated software or infrastructure.

## 5. Expected conduct

Everyone participating in the imPress community is expected to:

### 5.1 Communicate respectfully

- Treat others with patience, courtesy, and good faith, including during disagreements.
- Critique ideas, implementations, test evidence, and decisions rather than attacking the character, intelligence, identity, or presumed motives of a person.
- Use clear and inclusive language. Respect people's stated names and forms of address; ask rather than assume when clarification is appropriate.
- Recognize that written messages can be interpreted differently across cultures and communication styles. Request clarification before attributing harmful intent when circumstances allow.
- Avoid escalating tense exchanges. Where useful, summarize the technical disagreement, identify unresolved questions, and invite independent review.
- Respect boundaries and requests to stop unwanted personal contact.

### 5.2 Collaborate constructively

- Keep issues and reviews centered on reproducible problems, design constraints, relevant evidence, and practical next steps.
- Describe why a change is needed, what it affects, and how it was tested. Do not claim tests, approvals, or security reviews that did not occur.
- Provide actionable feedback, with specific examples or suggested improvements whenever possible.
- Welcome questions, especially from new contributors, and point people toward existing documentation without ridicule or gatekeeping.
- Give appropriate credit for work and ideas. Respect authorship, intellectual-property rights, third-party licenses, and attribution obligations.
- Resolve merge conflicts, design disputes, and responsibility for shared components through documented processes rather than personal pressure or unilateral sabotage.
- Accept that maintainers may reject a contribution for legitimate reasons, including scope, maintainability, security, compatibility, quality, or insufficient testing. Such decisions should be explained professionally.

### 5.3 Protect systems and information

- Use project access only for authorized purposes and follow the principle of least privilege.
- Never expose passwords, API tokens, signing keys, private certificates, session data, confidential configuration, or other credentials in commits, logs, screenshots, issues, or discussions.
- Minimize collection and sharing of personal information. Use synthetic, anonymized, or properly de-identified examples where possible.
- Report suspected vulnerabilities through a suitable private security channel rather than publishing sensitive exploit details that could create preventable harm.
- Treat classroom and institution-related information as potentially sensitive, even when it is technically accessible within a test or deployment environment.
- Keep test, staging, and production environments clearly separated, and obtain authorization before accessing, modifying, or exporting live records.

### 5.4 Respect access and accessibility needs

- Make reasonable efforts to communicate in accessible formats, with meaningful descriptions and sufficient context.
- Do not mock communication differences, spelling, language proficiency, disability, or a person's need for clarification or accommodation.
- When discussing accessibility defects, prioritize their effect on affected users rather than dismissing them as cosmetic or optional.

## 6. Unacceptable conduct

The following conduct is prohibited in spaces covered by this policy. This list is illustrative, not exhaustive.

### 6.1 Harassment and discriminatory behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, bullying, or deliberate humiliation.
- Slurs, derogatory stereotypes, demeaning remarks, or exclusion directed at a person's identity, background, disability, age, appearance, education, or experience.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, sexualized comments or imagery, coercive advances, or repeated inappropriate personal contact.
- Deliberate misgendering or repeated use of a name or form of address after a reasonable correction.
- Encouraging others to target or harass a person, including through coordinated messages or public pile-ons.
- Repeated unwanted contact after a clear request to stop.

### 6.2 Abuse of community processes

- Personal attacks disguised as code review, appeals to rank or popularity intended to silence others, or retaliatory rejection of contributions.
- Persistent off-topic disruption, spam, flooding, bad-faith issue creation, or repeated attempts to derail discussion after moderation guidance.
- Impersonation, fabricated evidence, deliberate misquotation, or misleading representation of another participant's position.
- Knowingly false accusations made to punish someone or manipulate enforcement. A report made in good faith is not a violation simply because the concern cannot be substantiated.
- Retaliation against reporters, witnesses, reviewers, people who cooperate with an inquiry, or anyone subject to an enforcement process.

### 6.3 Security, privacy, and operational abuse

- Doxxing or threatening to reveal personal contact details, private communications, location data, credentials, or other sensitive information without authorization.
- Unauthorized access to accounts, repositories, institutional systems, classrooms, devices, or deployment environments.
- Intentional compromise or sabotage of branches, CI pipelines, build artifacts, releases, update systems, dependencies, databases, signing processes, or project infrastructure.
- Deliberately introducing hidden functionality, malicious code, backdoors, credential exfiltration, or unsafe configurations.
- Publishing actionable details of an unmitigated vulnerability, confidential proof-of-concept material, or exploitable operational information without appropriate authorization or a responsible disclosure process.
- Falsifying security properties, compatibility claims, test outcomes, audit findings, review approvals, benchmark data, or deployment status.
- Misusing trusted access to alter student responses, teacher records, permissions, attendance-related information, device enrollment, or other operational data.

### 6.4 Other harmful conduct

- Deliberate obstruction of legitimate project work or misuse of administrative privileges.
- Instructions or encouragement to engage in prohibited conduct.
- Repeated refusal to follow reasonable, clearly communicated safety or moderation directions.

Firm criticism of architecture, code quality, maintainership decisions, release management, security claims, or project governance is not harassment merely because it is critical or uncomfortable. Participants may challenge decisions and request evidence. The expectation is to do so without threats, personal attacks, harassment, or deliberate disruption.

## 7. Standards for technical contributions

Professional conduct also includes accurate and responsible engineering practices. These expectations apply to code, documentation, automated workflows, configuration, tests, and release materials submitted to the official project.

### 7.1 Honesty and reproducibility

A contributor should distinguish among **implemented**, **tested**, **reviewed**, **verified**, **proposed**, and **untested** behavior. Pull-request descriptions and release notes should not imply a level of assurance that the available evidence does not support. When a defect is discovered after a merge, disclose it promptly through the appropriate channel and help document remediation.

### 7.2 Respect for review and change control

Do not bypass required reviews, branch protections, approval rules, or release checks for convenience. Do not rewrite shared history, force-push collaborative branches, modify another contributor's work destructively, or merge disputed changes without authorization. Legitimate emergency procedures may be used when documented and authorized.

Reviewers should distinguish blocking issues from preferences, communicate requested changes clearly, and avoid withholding approval for personal reasons. Contributors should respond professionally to substantive review feedback and may request another reviewer when a dispute cannot be resolved.

### 7.3 Documentation and maintenance

Document material behavior changes, relevant assumptions, migration steps, operational risks, and known limitations. Do not intentionally leave misleading instructions, conceal incompatible changes, or misrepresent whether a feature is suitable for production use.

### 7.4 Research, testing, and disclosure

Only test infrastructure, devices, accounts, and data for which you have permission. Use minimal-impact security testing and stop when a test threatens availability, confidentiality, integrity, or participant safety. The project welcomes responsible security research but does not authorize intrusion into third-party systems or classrooms.

## 8. Special responsibilities for classroom technology

Because imPress is designed for educational environments, project participants and operators should exercise particular care with information and systems involving students, educators, and institutions.

- **Student and educator privacy:** Avoid exposing names, identifiers, contact details, response histories, class rosters, authentication records, or information that could identify participants when combined with other data.
- **Consent and authority:** Access to a repository does not imply permission to access a school's, university's, teacher's, or student's data. Deployment operators must obtain the permissions appropriate to their environment.
- **Demonstrations and bug reports:** Prefer synthetic data. Redact private information from logs, screen recordings, screenshots, crash reports, and issue attachments before publication.
- **Testing and diagnostics:** Do not use real classroom data unnecessarily. Logs and telemetry should be limited to information needed for legitimate operations and troubleshooting.
- **Connected devices and updates:** Treat device identities, enrollment information, firmware distribution, update credentials, and remote-management functions as security-sensitive. Do not perform disruptive tests or updates on live classroom equipment without approval.
- **Responsible vulnerability handling:** Report authentication bypasses, privilege escalation, data exposure, unsafe update behavior, and similar findings privately so affected operators can evaluate and mitigate the risk.
- **Institutional requirements:** Deployers are responsible for determining and meeting the privacy, security, accessibility, and other legal or institutional obligations that apply to their own operations.

These expectations describe responsible behavior; they are not a guarantee that any version of imPress is secure, compliant with a particular law, or suitable for deployment without additional review. The project's documentation and security guidance should be consulted separately.

## 9. Reporting a concern

If you experience or observe conduct that may violate this Code of Conduct, you may report it without first confronting the person involved.

### 9.1 Choose an appropriate channel

1. **Immediate danger:** Prioritize safety and contact appropriate local emergency services or the relevant institution's emergency contact. Project maintainers do not provide emergency response.
2. **Community conduct:** Use a private contact channel that a repository owner or maintainer has actually published, if one is available. Do not post confidential allegations or identifying information in a public issue. GitHub does not guarantee that every account accepts private messages.
3. **Security vulnerabilities:** Follow the instructions in `SECURITY.md`, where available. A vulnerability report and a conduct complaint may be separate matters and may require different responders.
4. **Conflict of interest:** If a report concerns a maintainer, use a private channel published by another uninvolved owner or maintainer, if available. If no such channel is available, use GitHub's reporting process for content hosted on GitHub. Do not assume a private contact method exists.

The project does not publish a dedicated private conduct-reporting address in
this document. Do not send a sensitive report to an address or account that the
project has not actually designated for that purpose. If you cannot find an
available private route, use GitHub's report-abuse or safety process for the
relevant GitHub-hosted content; for an urgent safety risk, contact the
appropriate local emergency service or institution.

### 9.2 What to include

A useful report may include:

- A concise description of what happened and why it caused concern.
- Links to relevant project content and approximate dates or times.
- The involved accounts or participants, if known.
- Relevant context, screenshots, messages, or other evidence that you are authorized and comfortable sharing.
- Any immediate safety, privacy, or operational risks.
- Whether you request a reply, a confidential conversation, avoidance of contact, or another practical protective measure.

You do not need to provide every item. Share only what is necessary and avoid unnecessarily circulating other people's private information. A trusted person may assist with preparing a report.

Reporters and witnesses are not required to conduct their own investigations, attempt confrontation, collect evidence at personal risk, or gain unauthorized access to anyone's accounts or devices.

## 10. How reports are reviewed

Maintainers will seek to acknowledge reports as soon as reasonably practical and assess whether interim steps are needed. Responses depend on the urgency and complexity of the concern and the availability of appropriate reviewers. The project does not promise a fixed response time unless it publishes and can maintain one.

The review process should follow these steps where practical:

1. **Initial assessment:** Determine whether the report concerns project scope, immediate safety, sensitive data, platform abuse, or a security vulnerability that requires separate handling.
2. **Conflict screening:** Assign an uninvolved reviewer when reasonably available. A person directly implicated should not control the review of allegations against themselves.
3. **Information gathering:** Examine relevant context and request only the additional information needed to make a fair decision.
4. **Opportunity to respond:** Where safe and appropriate, allow the person whose conduct is reported to respond to the material concerns without exposing confidential sources unnecessarily.
5. **Decision:** Evaluate the nature, severity, frequency, intent where reasonably established, impact, prior relevant conduct, and risk of recurrence.
6. **Protective or corrective action:** Choose a proportionate response using only powers available to the maintainers or project administrators.
7. **Communication and recordkeeping:** Inform affected parties of the outcome to the extent appropriate, retain a limited internal record where necessary, and restrict access to that record.

Reports will be handled as discreetly as practical. Absolute confidentiality and anonymity cannot be guaranteed, particularly where disclosure is required to protect people, respond fairly, comply with law, or involve a platform or institutional authority. Maintainers should explain necessary information sharing when safe and feasible.

An unresolved or unsubstantiated allegation is not proof of misconduct. Equally, incomplete evidence does not require maintainers to ignore a credible immediate risk. Temporary precautions may be appropriate while facts are assessed.

## 11. Enforcement and corrective action

Maintainers may take action within official imPress spaces and project-controlled resources. Actions should be fair, proportionate, documented where appropriate, and consistent with project permissions and platform rules.

Possible measures include:

| Level | Action | Typical purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Informal guidance** | Clarify a boundary, request more constructive language, or redirect a discussion. |
| 2 | **Formal warning** | Identify a violation, document expectations, and explain potential consequences of recurrence. |
| 3 | **Content moderation** | Remove or edit harmful material where permitted, hide comments, lock threads, or close disruptive discussions. |
| 4 | **Temporary participation restriction** | Limit posting, reviewing, access, or participation for a defined period when available tools permit. |
| 5 | **Role or access removal** | Revoke project roles, repository privileges, or access to project-managed infrastructure. |
| 6 | **Long-term or permanent exclusion** | Prevent further participation in project-controlled spaces in cases of severe, repeated, or unresolved harmful conduct. |

These are possible responses, not a mandatory sequence. Serious threats, credential misuse, targeted harassment, or active security abuse may justify immediate protective measures. An immediate measure should be reassessed when practical and should not be represented as a final factual finding before an appropriate review.

When deciding on a response, maintainers should consider the conduct's impact, whether it was repeated, the seriousness of any safety risk, whether the participant understood prior guidance, and whether a less restrictive step can adequately protect others. Status, friendship, authorship, and volume of contributions should not provide immunity.

Enforcement under this document concerns official project participation. It does not automatically prevent someone from exercising rights granted under the software license in an independent environment. Legal rights, institutional discipline, platform enforcement, and code-license obligations are separate matters governed by their respective authorities.

## 12. Protection against retaliation

Retaliation is prohibited. Examples include threatening a reporter, revealing someone's identity to punish them for reporting, pressuring witnesses to withdraw statements, excluding people from collaboration solely for cooperating with a review, or using technical access to punish a person involved in a complaint.

Participants may raise good-faith concerns, request clarification, provide evidence, and appeal a decision without facing punishment for exercising those options. This protection does not excuse fabricated evidence, harassment, security abuse, or other independent misconduct.

Maintainers should take reasonable steps to prevent retaliation and may treat it as a separate violation even when the original report remains unresolved.

## 13. Review and appeals

A person directly affected by an enforcement decision may request a private review. The request should identify the decision, explain the reason for disagreement, and provide any relevant new context or evidence.

Where reasonably available, a maintainer who was not the sole original decision-maker should review the matter. If no independent maintainer exists, the project should acknowledge that limitation rather than promise independent review it cannot provide. Platform-level restrictions may need to be appealed through the platform's own process.

An appeal does not automatically suspend a safety-related restriction. A reviewer may uphold, adjust, or reverse the decision and should communicate the outcome privately when appropriate. Repeated appeals without new information may be closed after a reasonable review.

## 14. Maintainer duties and limitations

Maintainers hold additional responsibilities because they can shape participation and control project resources. They are expected to:

- Model respectful communication and apply the same rules to themselves.
- Explain important technical, contribution, and moderation decisions with reasonable clarity.
- Avoid conflicts of interest and, when possible, refer complaints involving themselves to an uninvolved person.
- Handle reports promptly relative to their urgency while avoiding premature conclusions.
- Limit access to sensitive reports, credentials, and personal information.
- Use repository permissions responsibly and maintain an understandable record of significant enforcement decisions where privacy allows.
- Avoid using moderation powers to silence legitimate security concerns, technical criticism, or good-faith disagreement.
- Correct inaccurate public statements and restore access or content when a decision is reversed and restoration is feasible.
- Make reporting routes discoverable and keep relevant security and contribution documents reasonably current.

Maintainers are volunteers or collaborators unless stated otherwise. This policy does not create an emergency support service, a legal representation relationship, or a promise of uninterrupted moderation. Actions beyond project control may require GitHub, deployment operators, institutional administrators, or relevant authorities.

## 15. Responsible use of project identity

Do not falsely claim to speak for imPress, impersonate its maintainers, represent a personal fork as an official release, or imply that an independent deployment is endorsed by the project without authorization.

When discussing the project publicly, distinguish personal opinions from official positions. Contributors may describe their own work accurately, but should not disclose restricted information or make unsupported claims about project security, performance, certification, or institutional endorsement.

Project names, logos, attribution, and license notices are distinct from this conduct policy and must be handled according to applicable project documentation and law.

## 16. Relationship to other project policies

This Code of Conduct should be read alongside applicable repository documents, which may include:

- `CONTRIBUTING.md` for contribution workflow, review conventions, and development requirements.
- `SECURITY.md` for vulnerability reporting and coordinated security handling.
- `LICENSE` for permissions and obligations associated with copying, modifying, and distributing software.
- Repository governance or maintainer documentation, where present, for project decision-making and administrative roles.

In the event of uncertainty, this document governs interpersonal participation in official project spaces, while the applicable license governs software rights. A technical contribution guideline should not be used to excuse harassment, and this Code of Conduct should not be interpreted as a substitute for legal, security, or deployment requirements.

## 17. Questions, revisions, and version history

Non-sensitive questions may be raised in an appropriate project discussion or
through a maintainer contact channel that is actually available. Do not post
private conduct reports or identifying information publicly. Suggestions to
improve this policy's clarity, accessibility, or fairness may be raised through
a project discussion or pull request.

The policy may be revised as the project evolves. Material changes should be reviewed, committed to the repository, and communicated through normal project channels. The current published version applies to conduct occurring after its publication, subject to platform rules and any applicable legal requirements. Maintainers should avoid imposing new standards retroactively without a compelling safety reason.

When adapting this document for an independent fork or organization, operators should identify their own reporting contacts, enforcement authority, and locally applicable responsibilities rather than implying that the original imPress team administers their community.

---

**A shared standard:** Every participant contributes to the quality of imPress not only through the code they write, but also through how they review, communicate, handle information, and respond when something goes wrong.
